Kestrel TKL Mechanical Keyboard: Eight Weeks of Daily Typing

The Kestrel TKL arrives sounding like a keyboard that costs twice as much, and eight weeks later it still does. The gasket-mounted plate and factory-lubed linear switches produce a deep, muted thock with almost no spring ping, and the foam stack under the keycaps and PCB damps the hollowness you hear in cheaper boards. Hot-swap sockets gripped every three-pin and five-pin switch we tried, and swapping took minutes without tools. The keycaps are PBT with dye-sub legends that show no shine after two months of full workdays, and the TKL layout leaves mouse room without stranding the arrow keys awkwardly. Wired and 2.4 GHz connections both polled flawlessly; Bluetooth handled three devices with one soft press of a function row shortcut. South-facing LEDs clear aftermarket keycaps, and per-key lighting is bright without washing out the legends. Battery life ran 21 days with lighting off, seven with it on, and the board charges over USB-C while working. Software is the weak spot: the driver is web-based, needed a reconnect quirk on first launch, and remapping layers is clunkier than the best competitors. Stabilizers were tuned well out of the box, with a faint tick on the left shift that a drop of lube cured. The aluminum case edges are chamfered cleanly, the knob on the top right controls volume and skipped nothing, and the typing angle suits both flat and raised preference. For anyone ready to leave prebuilt boards behind, start here.
