Grantwood Electric Standing Desk Review: A Year of Daily Use

After a full year on the Grantwood electric standing desk, it has earned its footprint in our office. The dual-motor lift is the headline feature, and it holds up: it raises an 80-kilogram load of monitors, a laptop dock, and a small library of reference books without a groan, and the four memory presets return to the same height within a millimeter every time. Transit speed is brisk at about 38 millimeters per second, quiet enough that a colleague on a call beside us never noticed a change. The desktop itself is a solid bamboo slab with a hard-wearing matte finish that has shrugged off a year of coffee rings and pen marks. Cable management is better thought out than most, with a magnetic tray under the rear lip and a grommet on each side. Assembly took us about 35 minutes with the included hex driver, and the instructions were refreshingly clear. Stability is excellent up to roughly 110 centimeters; above that, a light typing wobble appears, which is normal for a desk this size. The controller has a USB-C charging port and a child lock, and an anti-collision sensor reverses the desk if it meets an obstacle, which saved a chair more than once. Downsides are few. The bamboo is heavy, so plan for two people at setup, the preset buttons are unlabeled and take a week to memorize, and the warranty registration pushed a newsletter we did not want. At its usual sale price it undercuts the better-known brands while matching them on lift quality. For anyone splitting the day between sitting and standing, the Grantwood is a confident, durable recommendation.
