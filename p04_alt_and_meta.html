Fjellmark HEPA Air Purifier: Tested Through Wildfire Season

We ran the Fjellmark HEPA purifier through a month of wildfire smoke, and it kept a 40-square-meter room readable on our particle counter. The pre-filter spins off for a rinse, the HEPA cartridge seats with a quarter turn, and the replacement indicator is based on actual runtime rather than a dumb calendar, which we verified by resetting it. On its lowest setting the unit is a genuine whisper, the quietest we have measured in this size class, and sleep mode dims the control ring fully. Auto mode reacted to a burnt breakfast within 90 seconds, ramping through speeds we could track on the display, though its highest setting is loud enough that you will want the memory function instead. The app is optional and mercifully unobtrusive, with graphs of indoor readings and filter life, and no account was required for basic control. Energy use at the medium setting measured 28 watts, cheap enough to run all winter. Build quality is a step above the plastic-box norm: a steel shroud, rubberized feet, and a cord that routes either side. Our complaints are minor. The tinted smoke sensor needed a clean after the smokiest week, the top handle is narrow for gloved hands, and replacement filters cost more than rivals. Against a true smoke emergency the Fjellmark pulled the room back to baseline in under two hours, which is the number that matters. For allergy sufferers and anyone downstream of summer fires, it is an easy recommendation at its street price.
