Ridgeline Standing Desk: One Year of Sitting Less

A year in, the Ridgeline standing desk has made sitting less feel like a habit instead of a chore, and its motor is the reason. Two-stage legs rise from 61 to 127 centimeters in about 14 seconds, with a hum quiet enough for shared calls, and four memory presets meant everyone in the household actually used them. The bamboo top arrived well sealed, with no sharp edges and only one small knot, and the cable tray swallowed two power strips with room to spare. Stability is the spec that matters, and the Ridgeline holds its own: at full height with two monitors there is a faint wobble when typing hard, noticeably better than the budget frame we retired. The control puck includes a USB port and a child lock, and anti-collision sensitivity caught a rolling chair twice during testing. Assembly took two people 50 minutes; the instructions were picture-only and one step was ambiguous, but a QR video filled the gap. Over twelve months the surface picked up two light scratches and the finish darkened slightly where wrists rest, both normal wear. Our gripes: the presets reset during a two-week power unplug, the desk ships without a grommet cover, and the frame only comes in one gray. The lifetime frame warranty and five-year motor cover are among the best in the category, which matters for something used hundreds of times a year. If you want an electric desk that will still feel solid in year five, this is the one we point friends toward.
