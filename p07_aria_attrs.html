Keela Trail 30 Backpack Review: A Season on the Hills

Rated four stars out of five by our test team.

The Keela Trail 30 is a day pack that punches above its 30-liter class, and a season of hill walking has left us fond of it. The back panel is where the money went: a ventilated foam frame holds the load off your spine, and after a six-hour ridge walk in warm weather our shirt was noticeably drier than with our old pack. The hip belt is slim but takes real weight off the shoulders, and the load-lifter straps actually do something, pulling the mass in close on a scramble. Capacity is honest, swallowing a waterproof, a stove, lunch for two, and a camera with room to spare, and the roll-and-clip lid adds a few liters when you overpack. Materials feel built to last: a ripstop body with a stiffer base, chunky zips with grippy pulls, and bar-tacked stitching at every stress point. We like the twin stretch side pockets, which hold a bottle each and are reachable on the move, and the internal key clip is a small kindness. Niggles exist. The hydration sleeve is a tight fit for a three-liter bladder, the rain cover is sold separately, and the only color our shop stocked was a very loud orange. Comfort also drops off past about 12 kilograms, which is fair for the size but worth knowing. For fast days and light overnights, the Trail 30 carries beautifully and should last many seasons. At its mid-range price it is the one we now reach for first.
