The Kirkwall Burr Grinder: Six Months of Daily Espresso

After six months of near-daily use, the Kirkwall Burr Grinder has earned its spot on our counter, but not without a few caveats worth knowing before you buy. The 40mm conical burrs grind quietly enough to hold a conversation over, and the 40-step adjustment dial is a genuine step up from the stepped hoppers on cheaper machines. Espresso settings from 14 to 19 clicks produced repeatable shots, and the portafilter cradle did not scatter grounds the way our old unit did. Cleanup takes about two minutes: the upper burr lifts out without tools, and a stiff brush clears the chute. The hopper holds 300 grams, which is generous, though beans older than three weeks settled unevenly and forced small dial-ins. Where the grinder stumbles is volume. Beyond four doubles in a row, the motor smelled faintly warm, and the static on very dry winter beans was annoying enough that we started misting them first. The plastic housing also flexes if you press down on the hopper, which makes the machine rock slightly on a granite counter. Still, at this price the cup quality is the story. Shots pulled against a cafe reference were consistently described as syrupy, with the chocolate notes the roaster promised. Filter grind was equally competent, and a coarse setting for cold brew came out clean with almost no fines. If you pull espresso every morning and want to stop thinking about grind, this is an easy recommendation. If you batch-prep for a household of heavy coffee drinkers, step up a class and pay for a bigger motor.
