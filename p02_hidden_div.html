Harlan Pro 14 Laptop Review: The Sweet Spot for Students

The Harlan Pro 14 lands in the awkward middle of the laptop market, and mostly wins there. It costs less than the premium ultrabooks yet feels sturdier than the budget shelf, with an aluminum lid, a hinge that holds its angle at any opening, and a keyboard deck with no flex at all. Our review unit paired 16 gigabytes of memory with a fast mid-range processor, and that combination ran a full student workload comfortably: lecture slides, a dozen research tabs, a stats package, and a streaming call at once. The 2.8K display is the reason to pick this over cheaper Harlans. Text is crisp, colors are accurate enough for casual photo edits, and a full charge lasted 10 hours 45 minutes in our browsing loop, or about two library sessions without a charger. The speakers produce surprisingly full low end for a 14-inch chassis, though they distort at maximum volume. Ports include two USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, and a microSD slot, so most accessories plug in directly. The webcam is serviceable, and the privacy shutter is genuinely stiff to slide, which we came to appreciate. Downsides are real but livable. The fans spin audibly during long exports, the charger is a proprietary barrel plus USB-C arrangement, and the trackpad clicks loudly in quiet rooms. Harlan also ships background software that nags about its own cloud backup, which took one uninstall to silence. If your budget sits between bargain bins and flagship prices, this is the configuration to buy.
