Pylos Cast Iron Skillet Review: One Pan, Many Years

The Pylos 26-centimeter cast iron skillet arrives pre-seasoned and ready to work, and after three months of near-daily cooking it has only improved. The factory seasoning was better than most, giving us a workable non-stick surface from the first fried egg, and regular use has layered it into a deep, glossy patina that now releases food with almost no fuss. Heat retention is the whole point of cast iron, and the Pylos delivers: a steak dropped onto the preheated surface held its sear without the temperature crash that ruins a crust on thinner pans, and the heavy base spread the heat evenly across an induction hob with no hot ring in the center. At just under two kilograms it is hefty but not unmanageable, and the long handle stays cool enough to grip for a moment, though we still reach for a cloth. A helper handle opposite makes lifting a full pan to the oven genuinely safe. The pour spouts on each side are well shaped and actually channel a sauce rather than dribbling it down the outside. Care is the usual cast iron routine: a hot rinse, a wipe, and a thin film of oil, and ours has shown no rust despite a busy sink. Gripes are few and expected. It is heavy for one-handed tossing, the surface arrives a little rough and smooths only with use, and the matte finish shows oil smears. For a pan that could outlive its owner at a fair price, the Pylos is a confident recommendation for any kitchen that cooks over real heat.
