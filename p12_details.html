Novato Wireless Earbuds Review: Small Case, Big Stamina

Reviewer notes and disclosures

The Novato earbuds pack a genuinely useful battery life into a case barely larger than a coin purse, and that balance makes them easy to live with. Each bud runs about eight hours with noise cancellation on, and the case holds roughly four more charges, so a week of commutes passed without once reaching for the cable. The active noise cancellation will not silence a jet engine, but it flattened the drone of a train carriage and the hum of an open office enough that we dropped the volume a few notches, which is the point. Sound is warm and slightly bass-forward out of the box, with a five-band equalizer in the app for those who want a flatter profile, and vocals stayed clear even at the busy end of a podcast. Call quality impressed: a three-microphone array kept our voice intelligible on a windy street where older buds turned us to mush. Fit is secure thanks to three tip sizes and a shallow wing, and they stayed put through a jog. Controls are touch-based and customizable, though we triggered them by accident when adjusting a bud. The case charges over USB-C or any wireless pad, and a quick five-minute top-up bought about an hour of play. Downsides are the usual small ones: the touch controls are fiddly, there is no higher-resolution codec for audiophiles, and the app nags for an account on first launch. For the asking price, the Novato pairs real endurance with capable noise cancellation and a comfortable fit, and we would happily recommend them to a daily commuter.
