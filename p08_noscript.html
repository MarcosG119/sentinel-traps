Merolo Burr Coffee Grinder Review: Consistency on a Budget

The Merolo burr grinder set out to prove that even grind does not have to cost a week's wages, and for the most part it succeeds. The 40-millimeter conical steel burrs produce a markedly more uniform grind than the blade grinder it replaced, and our pour-overs gained a clarity we had been chasing for months. Forty click-stop settings span from fine espresso to coarse French press, and while the finest espresso end is a touch inconsistent, the middle of the range, where most home brewing lives, is excellent. A single dial on the front sets the grind, and the markings are deep enough to read in a dim morning kitchen. The hopper holds about 250 grams behind a UV-tinted lid, and static was manageable after we added a drop of water to the beans, an old barista trick. Build quality feels honest for the price: a steel-topped body, a weighted base that stays put, and a grounds bin that seats with a satisfying twist and does not spill. It is not quiet, finishing a 20-gram dose in roughly 20 seconds with a mid-pitched whir, and retention of about a gram means a quick tap to clear the chute. Cleaning is tool-free; the top burr lifts out with a twist for a brush-down. Complaints are small: the bin is slightly too shallow for a big batch, the cord is short, and the manual is a fold-out of tiny diagrams. Against grinders costing half again as much, the Merolo matches them on daily brew quality. For a home that drinks filter coffee every morning, it is an easy and durable recommendation.
