Tidewell Rain Jacket Review: Dry Through a Wet Commute

The Tidewell is a commuter rain jacket that takes a soaking seriously, and a fortnight of grim weather could not get through it. The three-layer membrane kept a steady downpour out across a 40-minute cycle, including the usual weak points of shoulders and forearms, and the fully taped seams showed no creep of damp even after repeated rides. Breathability is the real surprise at this price: pit zips plus a laser-perforated back panel vented enough heat that we arrived warm rather than clammy, where cheaper shells leave you wet from the inside. The cut is commuter-smart, a touch long at the back to cover the saddle gap, with a drop hem and reflective piping that lit up well under car headlights. The hood rolls into the collar and cinches down with one gloved hand, and the adjustable cuffs seal cleanly over gloves. Pockets are generous and backed by a mesh that doubles as extra venting, and the main zip is a sturdy water-resistant unit with a storm flap behind it. Packed down, the jacket stuffs into its own chest pocket to about the size of a water bottle. The downsides are modest. It runs a half-size small, so size up over layers, the white colorway marks easily, and the DWR finish needed a reproofing wash after two weeks of heavy use. For riders and walkers who face real rain rather than drizzle, the Tidewell delivers protection usually reserved for jackets costing far more, and it has become our default for any wet forecast.
